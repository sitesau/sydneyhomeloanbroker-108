How to compare broker fit

Comparison should focus on process, authorisation and lender coverage. Ask whether the broker is licensed or authorised to provide regulated credit advice, how recommendations are recorded, and which loan features matter for the scenario. Membership and authorisation belong in the verification step, including checking MFAA or FBAA accreditation and the credit licence or credit representative authority behind the advice.

  • Which lender types are on the panel for this application?
  • What assumptions sit behind the borrowing capacity estimate?
  • What changes if the property, build scope or settlement timing changes?
  • What fees, commissions or product limits should be understood?

A single enquiry is presented as the starting point for a discussion with brokers who compare loans from banks, credit unions and specialist lenders. That is useful only when the borrower knows which lender types are being considered and why others are excluded.

A refinancer should bring the current loan balance, repayment amount, interest rate, loan features and reason for switching, because lower repayments, accessing equity and improving features can pull in different directions.

An investor should ask how rental assumptions, existing debts, repayment type and portfolio lending affect lender fit. A construction or renovation borrower should ask how progress payments are assessed and what documents are needed for a new build or knock-down-rebuild; approval timeframes and lender rules should be confirmed directly because they are not stated.
